I built Soma for questions I have while studying. It works with the diagram already on my screen.

Here, the direct route from A to F costs eleven. Going through the other nodes costs seven. Muse Spark reads the diagram, Wonder reviews the reading against the image, and Soma calculates the route.

The graph lifts off the page. Height shows the cost of reaching each node. Cut D-to-F, and the best route changes. F rises to nine. Restore the connection, and it falls back to seven. The printed slide still says seven; the drawing shows my changed experiment.

Here’s a different question: why does this engine need two turns?

Muse places an illustrative cutaway and chooses the teaching poses. Soma moves its parts. At three hundred and sixty degrees, the piston has returned to the top, but the cycle is only halfway through. The second turn completes the four strokes. Keeping the turn count visible makes that distinction easier to see.

Now a circuit. Both middle nodes are at eight volts, so there’s no current across R-five.

I ask when that current would flow, and which way. Muse chooses R-four to vary. Soma calculates the balance point at eight ohms.

Changing that resistor in the panel updates the drawing on the slide. Below eight ohms, B is above C and current flows from B to C. At eight, they balance. Above eight, C is higher and the current reverses.

That’s what I want while studying: a way to try the question on the diagram itself and see what changes.
