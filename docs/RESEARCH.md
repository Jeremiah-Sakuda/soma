# Soma research and competitive context

**Research carried into the October 26, 2026 handoff.** This is a digest of earlier desk research and the supplied product discussion. It is not a hands-on competitive test, a new verification of every linked page, or evidence of product demand. Recheck relevant features and account access when running the comparison.

## What is already known about the problem

Jeremy reports using the consumer Muse assistant to follow Blackboard, Gradescope, and email, with browser access when no connector exists. Notion and Granola supply task and lecture context. The firsthand gap is translating that changing context into useful current practice and retaining learning evidence.

Other students sharing that recurring burden is a hypothesis. Observe real preparation tasks and correction effort; do not count interview targets or synthetic accounts as validation.

## Advantages to test, not assume

**External change to useful activity:** Does Soma require less repeated finding, copying, uploading, prompting, correction, and verification after a new lecture, scope change, or returned mistake? Can it apply the appropriate consequence while preserving valid existing work?

**Quality of the interactive relationship:** Does the activity reveal the specific course concept through meaningful controls, with checked calculation and supported explanation? Does a fresh question require applying that relationship? A visually polished animation can still fail this test.

**Evidence that affects what follows:** Does the system preserve assistance, novelty, versions, and actual delay accurately, then change its interpretation or next action? A permanent “mastered” badge and a source-grounded graph are not evidence of this behavior on their own.

Use the test protocol in [Demo and Evaluation](DEMO_AND_EVALUATION.md). Report parity, access constraints, and Soma's weaknesses. Do not turn untested hypotheses into claims that a competitor falls short.

## Other relevant references

[Google's study notebooks description](https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/products/gemini-app/gemini-study-notebooks/) describes personalized lessons, diagnostic quizzes and progress-based recommendations. [Source documentation](https://support.google.com/gemininotebook/answer/16215270?hl=en) is a reference for importing and syncing behavior. A Notebook-style study tool is a valid product context, but a Meta-versus-Google framing does not replace the event rubric or user evidence.

[Anatomy](https://github.com/wheresryan22/anatomy) and the article supplied by Jeremy inform the model-first visual approach: meaningful controls, a visible mechanism, shared numerical state, and one clear idea. Inspect the actual code and license before reuse. It is a build-time resource, not proof of reliable deployed generation or educational efficacy. Keep required notices.

[ts-fsrs](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/ts-fsrs) is the proposed scheduler. It schedules card review; it does not establish that a student understands a course.

[Meta's model documentation](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/models), [computer use](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/computer-use), [tool calling](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/tool-calling), and [image understanding](https://dev.meta.ai/docs/image-understanding) are implementation verification entry points. Confirm currently available models and exact contracts before coding against them. The consumer Muse product and a standalone application remain separate systems.

## Learning rationale and limits

[The Dunlosky review](https://www.psychologicalscience.org/publications/journals/pspi/learning-techniques.html) motivates practice testing and distributed practice. [Bastani and colleagues](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2422633122) motivate measuring unassisted performance separately from supported completion.

These sources inform the design; they do not prove Soma improves learning. Record actual independent attempts, compare controlled questions when feasible, and treat a small hackathon pilot as exploratory. Engagement, source grounding, computational consistency, and long-term learning are different claims.
