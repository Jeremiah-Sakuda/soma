# Soma student observation kit

Status: protocol ready; no students tested by creating this document. Aim for 5–8 voluntary participants before submission if available. Small, self-selected observations cannot establish long-term learning gains.

## Ten-minute session

1. Ask the participant to choose a readable slide or problem they may share. Avoid private messages, grades, credentials and third-party student records. Explain that an explicitly submitted screenshot goes to Meta; the saved artifact retains derived text, not its raw pixels.
2. Before opening Soma, ask what they want to understand and how they would normally investigate it. Record familiarity and prior AI-tool use. Do not prime them with a prepared graph answer.
3. Have them capture/upload, ask, explore and make a follow-up with minimal coaching. Record first-request success, waiting time, corrections, help given, confusing controls and source/explanation errors. Preserve failures in the denominator.
4. For supported graphs, ask them to change one cost/connection and explain the result. For other subjects, ask which part of the explanation helped or misled them. Do not imply arbitrary checked simulation.
5. Ask them to save/reopen and continue. Ask what they would use again and what would prevent them returning. Obtain explicit permission before quoting them; anonymize by default.

## Record per participant

Use an anonymous ID, subject, familiar/unfamiliar topic, device, source type, first request result, generation seconds, retries, independent source-fidelity review, task completion, help, save/resume result, observed confusion and a permissioned exact quote. Store actual private participant material locally, outside Git/public assets; report only aggregate findings and consented quotations.

Report numerator and denominator separately for requests and people. A retry is not a first-request success. Separate prompted completion from voluntary reuse. Say “4 of 6 completed capture-to-save without help,” if observed, rather than “students learn faster.” Comparing preparation effort needs the same task and a recorded baseline, not a retrospective time-saving estimate. Later independent retention requires fresh questions and actual elapsed time.

## Submission-ready statement template

“We observed [N] students on their own [source types]. [n/N] completed [defined task] without help; [n/requests] first requests produced a usable explanation. We observed [specific failure] and changed [specific behavior]. This is early usability evidence, not a learning-outcome study.” Fill only with recorded observations.
