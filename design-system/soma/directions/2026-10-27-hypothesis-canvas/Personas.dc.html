Step back · who opens Soma, and why

The graph is the premise. The next action is the home.

Four of five people open Soma to answer “what now?”, not to browse a map. So the map should explain every recommendation and sit one tap away, and Today should show the slice of the map that matters this week. Personas are hypotheses drawn from the PRD and user stories, not interview results.

The next activity with time chips and its station chip; a count of what changed; where you are this week on the map

One tap

Full map and exam scope · station evidence and correction · Ask (⌘K) · Activity

Recommended navigation

Desktop sidebar

  1. Today: the next action plus this week’s slice of the map
  2. Map: the whole semester; filter by course or exam
  3. Activity: what Soma checked, ran and changed

Then course lines (each opens the map filtered) and, pinned at the bottom, Sources with a freshness dot. ⌘K handles asking and jumping.

Phone tab bar

TodayMap＋ AskActivity

Sources live under the profile.

Why not Courses or Study tabs? A course is a line on the map, so a separate Courses area would duplicate it. Lessons open from wherever you are, so they don’t need a destination of their own. Activity earns a top-level spot because trust is Jordan’s reason to adopt Soma and the visitor’s proof that the agent really works.

Layout principles

  1. One primary action per screen, sized to the time you have.
  2. The graph explains; it never blocks. Every card carries its station chip, and the map is one tap away.
  3. One object everywhere. A station chip opens the same inspector on desktop or sheet on phone.
  4. Time is an input, not a calendar. 10, 20 or 45 minute chips set the plan; a commitment sets the default and says why.
  5. Honest by default. Who-said-what labels, “suspected” wording, synthetic-mode labels.
