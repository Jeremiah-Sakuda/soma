# Meta hackathon requirements for Soma

**Source:** the full participant guide supplied by Jeremy, preserved verbatim in [PARTICIPANT_GUIDE.txt](sources/PARTICIPANT_GUIDE.txt). This handoff records it on October 26, 2026. It is a planning digest, not a substitute for later official announcements or the complete governing terms.

## Entry and project fit

Soma is planned for **Indie Developer / Build for Your World**. The theme asks builders to bring Meta models into a firsthand domain, free time and mental space, and make something newly possible. The guide allows solo participants and teams of at most four.

Meta models must be used in the product. The guide names the Spark series, Wonder, Image, Voice Transcribe, SAM 3.1, Glimmer and Spark Open, and Muse Code for development. It does not impose a count of distinct models.

The guide explicitly discourages basic RAG and simple “AI for Education” chatbots. It does not state a blanket ban on education. Soma's evidence should be a useful autonomous sequence, actual model decisions, persistent prepared learning artifacts, and adaptation from attempts. A different label or a browser clip alone does not guarantee eligibility.

## Original work and public artifacts

Repositories must be public. The demo must highlight features, code, and functionality built during the event, and judges must be able to identify original contributions. The guide warns of disqualification if those contributions are not clear. It permits startups to build on existing products when newly developed work is identified.

Prepare a truthful contribution record separating event implementation, earlier planning assets, third-party dependencies, and original synthetic material. Retain required notices. Do not claim that using a build-time coding tool proves runtime Meta integration.

Use only code, data, and assets the project has rights to use. Public artifacts should use original synthetic coursework and permitted examples; private student sources, auth/session state, and learner records do not belong in the public repository.

The handoff does not publish a repository, grant additional access, or submit an entry. Prepare concrete reviewable artifacts before any final authorization that is needed.

## Submission checklist

- One project per team, with a representative and all contributors listed.
- Publicly accessible repository.
- Accessible **three-minute demo video** showing specific event-built functionality.
- Working live product/judge path; the guide identifies code, video, and live product as judging artifacts.
- Accurate original-contribution and model-use statements.
- Tested links in a clean browser session.
- Submission via the Cerebral Valley platform before November 4 at 9 PM.
- Actual receipt/confirmation recorded after submission.

Aim to have assets ready November 3. Do not assume a late upload will be accepted.

## Judging

Stage One is pass/fail viability, reasonable theme fit, and reasonable Meta API/open-weight use to build during the event. Stage Two scores qualifying entries from 0–10 against the weighted criteria below. The five highest-scoring entries per track advance to live final video ranking using the same rubric.

| Criterion | Weight | Soma evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Impact | 30% | Real-world potential, identifiable beneficiaries, significance, likelihood of use and problem-statement fit. Observed outcomes can support claims but are not required. |
| Meta tooling | 25% | Meaningful source investigation, multimodal interpretation, graph decisions and actual adaptive output |
| Technical depth and execution | 20% | Checked models, versioned evidence, isolation, recovery and measured generation reliability |
| Demo | 25% | Immediate manipulable lesson, real consequence of a source event, live judge interaction and clear evidence |

The rubric asks for creative use beyond basic integration. It does not say that three models score higher than one. Choose a second model only if it improves the demonstrated product.

Credits are distributed in batches; verify they actually appear in Billing. A valid payment method is required and the guide describes a temporary preauthorization.

## Awards and scope

Indie prizes are $150k, $100k, $75k, $50k and $25k. The main goal remains a strong Indie submission.

The guide also lists Best Agentic Use-Case ($5k), Best Build on Meta Open Weight Models ($10k), Best Creative Build using Image or Wonder ($5k), Most Viral Project ($20k), and Community Vote ($10k). The agentic award fits the existing concept. Other awards are optional; do not add models or features just to enter them.

The viral award involves an X demo post and submission of its link, with engagement counted through November 8 EOD. This is information, not authorization to post. Open-weight entries require an eligible deployment and verification. Community voting opens after the closing ceremony; the result is November 16.


### PM128 — Impact calibration correction, November1

The participant guide asks about real-world potential, beneficiaries, significance, likelihood of use and problem-statement fit. It does not require a learner study, measured learning improvement, adoption or quantified time savings. Earlier simulated judges over-weighted missing empirical outcomes and treated a possible source of supporting evidence as a scoring prerequisite. For a hackathon, assess the concrete problem, credible benefit, distinctive capability and working demonstration; distinguish that plausible impact from any claimed measured results. Preserve truthful limitations without imposing a startup-validation or research-study gate.
