## Product outcome

Soma connects course content and learning evidence, proposes explanations for a student's difficulty, and builds an interactive experiment that distinguishes those explanations. The student's response changes what the graph supports and what Soma prepares next.

The flagship is a graph that makes testable predictions and generates a useful activity from its uncertainty. Its visible payoff is an experiment tailored to a particular ambiguity in the student's work. The graph also retains the evidence needed to challenge Soma's interpretation.

| Capability | Required behavior |
| --- | --- |
| Connected concepts and evidence | Stable, course-scoped concept references connect sources, lesson objectives, observations and interpretations. Show the relevant local connections and their evidence in the existing lesson inspection surface. |
| Competing explanations | Spark proposes two plausible explanations for an ambiguous response, with evidence and limitations. It may instead request clarification when two defensible explanations cannot be formed. |
| Informative experiment | Spark selects approved controls, inputs, an observable and distinct predicted responses. The numerical model checks that the proposed comparison actually distinguishes the predictions. |
| Visible predictions | The lesson presents the competing predictions and the checked result with clear labels and shared units. Hypothetical predictions never masquerade as the scientific model or as established student beliefs. |

| Graph revision | A saved response can weaken, provisionally support or leave an interpretation unresolved. Preserve the earlier version, original observation and assistance. A student correction can trigger reconsideration without rewriting course facts. |
| Changed next activity | When the new evidence warrants it, Spark prepares a materially different supported diagnostic or lesson configuration. A changed status label alone does not demonstrate adaptation. Retaining valid work or uncertainty is also a supported outcome. |
| Fresh application | Offer a different problem or representation within the supported family. Save the actual response and conditions; success cannot automatically establish another concept or course as mastered. |

## The experience

1. Today has a ready activity with a source-linked reason. An applicable update or returned-work observation explains why it is here.
2. In the lesson, a compact “What Soma is checking” view presents the relevant concept, two provisional explanations and supporting observations. The student can inspect sources or correct the interpretation.
3. The interactive scene makes the disputed relationship tangible. The student can predict before revealing the checked outcome, or explore first with that assistance recorded. Displaying an explanation or answer-revealing hypothesis counts as exposure/support for the related response; the system cannot label it independent merely because the result animation was hidden.
4. A before/after explanation shows what evidence changed, which interpretation was revised and why the next activity follows. If the evidence is ambiguous, say so.
5. A fresh application checks the same relationship in a different condition. Saved history separates this observation from earlier supported work and any genuinely delayed attempt.

Use the existing Ready Card and lesson/why/evidence surfaces. The local connection view can use linked cards or a small accessible diagram; it is not a new full-screen graph destination. Every visible link should lead to evidence or an action. No force-directed semester canvas, new navigation hierarchy or arbitrary graph editor is required.

## What proves it works

Use the existing development and held-out evaluations; add these dimensions to their cases rather than counting reused examples as extra independent evidence.

- Before prediction reveal, two plausible explanations lead to distinguishable responses in the proposed experiment. Independently review whether they are pedagogically plausible; numerical distinguishability alone is insufficient.
- At least one contrary-response branch leads to a newly prepared supported follow-up with a different question or meaningful configuration, preserved after managed persistence/reload. Include an unchanged/ambiguous branch where forced adaptation would be wrong.
- Changing the student's response while holding the starting context fixed can change the interpretation or next action through actual Spark decisions. Do not encode the outcome in a demo scenario label.
- Source revisions invalidate affected proposals before publication. Corrections, retry, stale jobs and another account cannot overwrite or misattribute the original evidence.
- Reopening preserves records and reuses valid artifacts. A course change, response or correction triggers its authorized preparation/reconsideration path without an extra conversational instruction; queued work and completed work remain distinct.
- A fresh application has a different item/condition and truthful exposure/help records. Report transfer within the tested context, not general mastery or causal learning improvement.
- The local connection view, comparisons and controls work with keyboard, reduced motion and a readable text/numeric alternative.

Existing five-reference numerical checks, Day 3 development gate, 8/10 unseen supported generation gate, negative cases, returned-work evaluation and privacy/source gates remain unchanged. Text fallback does not count as a passed interactive flagship case.

## Stretch goals

**First stretch: one verified cross-course bridge.** With suitable sources from both courses, connect one shared relationship and offer a fresh application in the second context. Distinguish conceptual similarity from equivalence, retain each course's assumptions and test transfer rather than propagating mastery. Use the same checked family where an interactive representation is needed. Broader automatic discovery across all classes is the longer-term vision.

**Second stretch: specialized Muse evidence reasoning.** Follow [Learning Specialist Proposal](LEARNING_SPECIALIST_PROPOSAL.md): one task, one candidate open-weight model/adapter, reviewed demonstrations and an independent base-versus-tuned comparison. Its role is choosing plausible explanations and useful diagnostics. Training is conditional on a demonstrated baseline weakness, available data, verified serving path and an explicit training/serving budget. It is not necessary for the graph or flagship interaction to work, and it does not imply per-student weight updates.

**Later:** broad cross-disciplinary transfer discovery, richer learner-authored relationship editing, additional checked model families, and a semester-wide explorable graph. These remain outside the hackathon commitment.
