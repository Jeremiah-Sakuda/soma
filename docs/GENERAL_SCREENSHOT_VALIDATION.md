# Source evaluation

These are executed development samples, not population reliability estimates or evidence of improved learning. Original source images and first-request results remain in the linked fixtures. Answer keys were kept out of provider prompts. Now-disclosed cases are development evidence, not future held-out tests.

## Earlier cross-subject checks

The initial nine-source batch used three public pages and six original images, each with an opaque name, fresh session and the same generic question. Eight of nine first requests produced the intended explanation/clarification; an OpenStax photosynthesis page failed safely with no artifact. Two identical code layouts with different constants produced their own sequences (7→9→11 versus 11→14→17), rather than carrying facts across images. A later counter, water-cycle source and debugging screenshot also published, with bounded repair used on some turns. Fixtures are under `tests/fixtures/general-screenshots`.

A later eight-subject development batch initially published 0/8 because a provider null sentinel failed strict interpretation; after the narrow normalization repair, the unchanged set published 7/8. A subsequent ten-source repeat published 8/10: physics interpretation and astronomy review failed. Both later published on repeats, which do not replace their first failures. Attention and autoencoder sources also had first-request failures in a later small sample. A further six-source polish run first published 5/6 and later 6/6 on repetition. These different development batches must not be pooled into a current success rate.

Sources included [MDN closures](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/JavaScript/Guide/Closures), [Google gradient descent](https://developers.google.com/machine-learning/crash-course/linear-regression/gradient-descent), [OpenStax photosynthesis](https://openstax.org/books/biology-2e/pages/8-2-the-light-dependent-reactions-of-photosynthesis), [Python list comprehensions](https://docs.python.org/3/tutorial/datastructures.html#list-comprehensions) and [NASA orbital laws](https://science.nasa.gov/solar-system/orbits-and-keplers-laws/). Public-page captures used for development are not private-course fixtures. General explanatory diagrams remain fallible; successful publication does not certify every sentence or a scientific model.

Inputs and first-request results are in `tests/fixtures/exploration-source-evaluation/`. Each case had one outer request, with only the service's bounded internal repair. Thus 12/13 requests published the intended artifact/refusal category; 10/10 fully legible supported sources published. This small development evaluation is not a general success-rate estimate. The cases are now disclosed development evidence, not future held-out tests. Recorded provider cost for the 13 requests was $0.496014, including the failure. These are historical run costs, not the current remaining allowance.

## Second fresh source evaluation (November 2)

Twelve new original slides were rendered headlessly by `scripts/render-source-evaluation-v2.mjs` and frozen with their answer key before any provider call: SHA-256 `236e49854643b7579cb7b22cd37e7a05cb285f411d5e5afcc747cf34cb8187ed` over `cases.json` and every image. Inputs, key and results are in `tests/fixtures/source-evaluation-v2/`. Each case was one outer request on a fresh guest through the service, with Wonder review on and the hosted Firestore store; the answer key was never sent. A reading passed only if every printed label, kind, value and connection matched, each source had the right polarity, the ground was right, and Soma's solution of the reading matched the key: every node voltage for a circuit, the goal cost for a graph. An offline test first checks that every key solves and that the scorer rejects a flipped source, a value off by a factor of ten and a missing edge (`tests/providers/source-evaluation-v2.test.ts`).

- Six circuits, all read exactly and solved to the key's voltages: a kΩ series–parallel network; two batteries; a 5 mA current source with kΩ resistors; a ladder with no ground symbol, where ground is taken at the battery's − terminal; a six-node network with decimal values; and ground marked on the battery's + terminal, which puts the − terminal's node at −15 V.
- Five graphs of 7, 8, 9, 10 and 12 nodes, all read exactly with the key's goal cost: undirected, directed, decimal weights, an unweighted graph for breadth-first search, and a 12-node graph at Soma's limit.
- One RC circuit with a capacitor and a switch, which the checked solver does not cover. Spark correctly did not read it as a checked circuit and Wonder approved the explanation, but the explanation failed to save. The source carried an undefined layout field, which Firestore rejects. Since November 1 (`a082e82`) this affected every screenshot with no readable graph or circuit on the hosted store; the offline tests use a JSON store that drops the field, so they did not catch it. The undefined-field publication defect was fixed in `10d3d97` with a regression test. This is a historical failure, not a claim that its rerun succeeded. One rerun of this case after the fix again declined the circuit, but the explanation call reached the 30-second per-call limit (`results-rc-after-fix.json`). It is still not a successful explanation.

Thus 11 of 12 cases produced the intended outcome on the first run, and 11 of 11 supported slides were read exactly. A solved circuit took 28–46 s and 3–5 model calls; a graph took 38–69 s and 5–6 calls. Recorded provider cost for the first run was $0.93, including the failure. The rerun was charged its full $0.35 reservation because its usage was uncertain. Like the first set, these are now disclosed development cases rather than future held-out tests, and a small sample rather than a success rate.

## Third-party figures and the hosted app (November 3)

To test material we didn't draw, three figures from Wikimedia Commons were frozen with hand-read answer keys before any provider call (SHA-256 `81c387e89a016e5b95b19d5c56a5b85a6d5950cd2547dfbdc365f1adc6262022`; [attribution and licenses](../tests/fixtures/real-figures/ATTRIBUTION.md)) and run once each through the live service on the hosted store.

- A directed weighted graph (CC BY-SA 3.0) was read exactly, with Wonder confirming all 6 nodes and 7 weighted connections, and solved to the key's cost of 20.
- Wikipedia's classic Dijkstra graph (public domain), a 283-pixel animation frame enlarged 4×, published correctly: every connection and weight right, the route 1 → 3 → 6 → 5 at cost 20. Spark kept the figure's printed "a" and "b" beside nodes 1 and 5 in their names ("1 a", "5 b"), so strict label scoring fails; with those annotations stripped, the reading matches the key exactly. In this run Spark's first reading failed its typed contract and was re-read once, and Wonder sent the explanation back once before approving it. The harness itself crashed while scoring this case (its answer key used "1" as an ID), so the case is scored offline from the published artifact; a second run gave the same reading and cost ([results](../tests/fixtures/real-figures/results.json), [rerun](../tests/fixtures/real-figures/results-numbered-rerun.json)).
- A Kirchhoff circuit drawn with symbols and no values (CC BY-SA 4.0) got a clarifying question; Soma did not invent values.

Recorded provider cost was $0.25 for the three cases and $0.08 for the rerun. Three figures are a spot check, not a measure of reliability on coursework.

The hosted app at release `soma-circuits-review3-2026-11-03` was also checked headless: production's `release.json` is byte-identical to the source manifest; the Lecture 14 button drew the checked search in 49 s with Wonder's reading-check line; the Lecture 9 button drew the circuit in 45 s; and a circuit investigation chose R₄ and found exactly 8 Ω, verified by re-solving. Muse's stated reason for R₄ was dropped by a filter bug that a part's label at the end of a sentence defeated (fixed in `087f247`). After the fix, one investigation timed out when its composition call reached the 30-second per-call limit, as the capacitor rerun had; exploration calls now get up to 45 s within the unchanged 90-second turn (`8d4c313`). At release `soma-circuits-review6-2026-11-03` the hosted investigation then completed in 32 s and showed Muse's reason: "Changing R4 shifts node C relative to node B so R5 direction reveals imbalance."

## Interpretation of results

Across the two frozen original-image sets, all 13 supported graphs and 6 supported circuits were read to their stated keys; unsupported/ambiguous cases retain the two failed desired outcomes described above. This is narrow evidence on authored slides, not arbitrary coursework. Third-party figure results include a strict-label mismatch, a harness scoring failure and a rerun, as reported.

Independent numerical reference checks validate computation on the interpreted model. They cannot prove Spark read the source correctly or that Wonder’s review is complete. The [verification record](VERIFICATION.md) separates offline tests, browser rendering, hosted generation, packaged distribution and physical-device acceptance.
